Case Study 01 · Louis Vuitton Japan

Building Louis Vuitton Japan's
Graduate Employer Brand

A strategy-to-execution case study, in seven chapters. Flip through →

Overview

Louis Vuitton Japan recruits graduates through Japan's highly competitive Shinsotsu system, where leading companies compete simultaneously for the country's highest-potential university students. The objective was not simply to hire graduates. It was to build a long-term talent pipeline capable of developing future retail leaders.

I was responsible for developing the employer branding strategy, coordinating cross-functional initiatives, and leading the execution of key recruitment projects that strengthened Louis Vuitton's presence among Japan's most competitive graduate talent.

1 yrfull annual recruitment cycle, research through offers
6+stakeholder groups: HR, retail leadership, marketing partners, vendors, universities and candidates
A→Zstrategy, planning and execution owned end-to-end

The Challenge

Unlike many Western systems, Japanese graduate recruitment follows one structured annual cycle in which nearly every major employer competes for the same pool of students. The challenge was not attracting the most applicants. It was identifying and attracting the graduates with the highest long-term leadership potential.

That meant answering five questions with evidence, not assumptions:

  1. Who are our ideal future leaders?
  2. What characteristics do they share?
  3. Where can we find them?
  4. How should Louis Vuitton speak to them?
  5. Which recruitment channels create the greatest impact?

The objective was clear: strengthen the graduate employer brand, attract high-potential candidates, increase applications from target universities, improve efficiency through data-driven decisions, and support the company's long-term goal of increasing the proportion of managers in their twenties.

Research & Insight

Everything began with the audience. I built an ideal-graduate persona from the qualities shared by successful future leaders inside the organisation, focusing on behaviour rather than academic performance alone.

Candidate persona · anonymised

The Future Retail Leader

Leadership potential Communication Adaptability Curiosity Learning agility Luxury mindset Cultural fit Career motivation

I then analysed previous recruitment data: the source universities of successful hires, conversion rates, and channel performance. The analysis showed that students from a specific group of highly ranked universities were significantly more likely to match the persona.

This single insight became the foundation of the entire strategy.

Strategy

From the research I developed an annual recruitment roadmap that aligned every employer branding activity across the cycle and created one consistent brand experience from first touchpoint to offer.

Phase 01Research, data analysis & persona
Phase 02Website, content & recruitment marketing
Phase 03Events, webinars & career forums
Phase 04Selection support & candidate experience

Reflection & Key Learnings

“Attracting exceptional talent requires the same principles as consumer marketing: understand the audience, build a compelling brand, create meaningful experiences, and stay consistent at every touchpoint.”

  1. Talent attraction is consumer marketing. The discipline is the same: audience, brand, experience and consistency.
  2. Data beats assumptions. One analysis reshaped where every yen and hour was invested.
  3. Brands are built at every touchpoint. A website, a webinar and a person each carry the brand.
  4. Strategy only matters when execution follows. Coordination is what turns a framework into results.

Case Study 03 · Louis Vuitton Japan

Employer Branding
Through Storytelling

Repositioning graduate recruitment to connect with Generation Z.

The insight

“Our videos explained the company.
The competitors' videos sold a future.”

Overview

Louis Vuitton Japan's graduate program competes in one of the most competitive hiring markets in the world, with major employers recruiting from the same universities in the same season. The existing employer branding videos carried the elegance of the Maison, but I did not believe they connected with the next generation of talent. I wanted to shift the focus from what the company wanted to say to what students wanted to feel.

The Challenge

Our recruitment content was informative but not emotional: a text-heavy website and videos that showcased luxury without building a personal connection. Generation Z candidates responded to content that was shorter, more personal, visually engaging and career-focused. The challenge was modernising the storytelling without softening Louis Vuitton's luxury identity.

Before · information

After · story

One person. One message.

Strategy

Rather than longer, information-heavy videos, I proposed a series of short employee films, each built around a single message and shot like a movie introduction rather than a corporate interview.

Illustrative shot sequence, recreated for portfolio purposes

Planning

This project sat outside the existing recruitment budget, so gaining approval came first. I built a case comparing our employer branding against competitors, then sourced and evaluated production vendors against five criteria.

Creative style Production quality Deadline reliability Luxury brand fluency Budget flexibility

Creative Direction

Rather than featuring only senior leaders, I chose five employees whose career paths showed what was genuinely possible: three from retail, two who moved from retail into head office. I defined each person's core message and directed the visual style toward simplicity, letting each story carry the luxury rather than the production.

Launch

The finished films became reusable assets across the recruitment ecosystem rather than a one-off release.

Results

This campaign launched alongside the wider graduate employer branding strategy and contributed to its growth in applications (see Case Study 01 →), rather than driving that number alone. What this project uniquely produced was the format itself.

5 employee films, each built around one authentic message
1 reusable content format, adopted across future recruitment cycles
GenZ a modern storytelling approach without softening the luxury identity

Reflection

“I realised the work I enjoyed most was not recruitment administration. It was understanding audiences, finding the insight, and turning it into a story.”
